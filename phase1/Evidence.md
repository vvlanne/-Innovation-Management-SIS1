# Cagan Audit — Air Astana Group  
## Phase 1: Evidence Extraction

---

## 1. Missionaries vs Mercenaries

**Quote:**  
"OUR PURPOSE: Connecting Kazakhstan and the rest of Eurasia with true Kazakh hospitality.  
OUR STRATEGY: Growth, Efficiency, Excellence.  
OUR VALUES: Air Astana's HEART (Hospitable, Efficient, Active, Reliable, Trustworthy) and FlyArystan's CHARM (Creative, Happy, Agile, Reliable, Modern)."

**Page:** 101

**Assessment:**  
Shows articulated purpose and values framework, suggesting missionary orientation at a cultural level. However, this remains aspirational language; there is limited evidence in the provided text of empowered teams or problem-driven missions at the operational level.

---

## 2. Project vs Product Funding

**Quote:**  
"Board activities in 2024: Strategy: IPO preparations and listing on LSE, AIX, and KASE; approval of the 2025–2029 Business Plan; creation of a new ground handling entity.  
Risk Management: Approval of procurement procedures, aircraft leases (A321ceo, A320neo), and purchase of GEnx-1B74/75 engine; share repurchase program."

**Page:** 104

**Assessment:**  
Evidence of project-based and approval-driven capital allocation (discrete aircraft purchases, specific business plan approvals). There is no indication of continuous product funding models or empowered product teams with budget authority.

---

## 3. IT Service vs Product Mindset

**Quote:**  
"The industry is rapidly transforming digitally, with an electronic document-management system for cargo, an e-identity card system for passengers, and biometric identification."

**Page:** 28

**Assessment:**  
Digital initiatives are described as discrete system implementations (e-document, e-identity, biometrics) rather than as continuously evolving, customer-centric products. This suggests a service-delivery and implementation mindset.

---

**Additional Quote:**  
"We are constantly looking at ways to make operations even more efficient and turn costs into profit centres. We have just received our second full flight simulator, which will be in service by the year end. We are in the process of establishing our Ground Services subsidiary, Air Astana Terminal Services, to increase efficiency and potentially generate revenue by serving other airlines at our principal hubs at Almaty and Astana."

**Page:** 25

**Assessment:**  
Strong emphasis on operational efficiency, asset utilisation, and cost optimisation, rather than on product innovation or solving customer problems through digital products.

---

## 4. Output vs Outcome Focus

**Quote:**  
"In 2024, the Air Astana Group carried 9.0 million passengers, a growth of 11.2% year on year, generating USD 145.7 million operating profit on total revenues of USD 1.3 billion, an increase of 14.9% and 12.4% respectively on an underlying basis. Profit after tax was USD 65.2 million, a growth of 6.0% excluding non-recurring items."

**Page:** 24

**Assessment:**  
Financial outcomes are tracked comprehensively and reported clearly at the group level.

---

**Quote:**  
"The network continues to grow, with 21 new routes added in 2024, 14 of which launched in Q4. In 2025, we have already announced another eight new routes in key growth markets, including direct flights from Almaty to Frankfurt, Guangzhou, Mumbai, and Da Nang and Nha Trang in Vietnam."

**Page:** 25

**Assessment:**  
Output metrics (number of routes launched) are featured prominently alongside financial outcomes. This mixed focus suggests a tendency toward output counting rather than direct measurement of customer or operational outcomes.

---

## 5. Control / Risk-Oriented Governance

**Quote:**  
"In 2024, the Board of Directors held 20 meetings (11 in person) with 100% attendance...  
Risk Management: Approval of procurement procedures, aircraft leases (A321ceo, A320neo), and purchase of GEnx-1B74/75 engine; share repurchase program."

**Page:** 104

**Assessment:**  
The Board is deeply involved in operational and procurement-level decisions, indicating a centralised and control-oriented governance model rather than one that delegates autonomy to empowered teams.

---

**Quote:**  
"Day-to-day management of the business is delegated to the CEO, whose activity is supported by the Executive team... Under the JSC Law, transactions with related parties require the approval of the Board or shareholders."

**Page:** 101

**Assessment:**  
While operational responsibilities are formally delegated, significant board-level approval requirements remain in place, reinforcing a risk-averse and control-driven governance structure.
