# AI Transformation – Strategic Portfolio   
**Company:** Air Astana Group  
 

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project simulates the role of a **Head of Product Innovation** at Air Astana Group.  
The objective is to evaluate the organization’s readiness for AI-driven transformation and to design a **governed, financially justified AI agent** for critical internal operations.

The focus of the project is **not “AI for AI’s sake”**, but AI as a **decision-support tool** that improves operational efficiency while preserving accountability, regulatory compliance, and human judgment.

---

## 2. Problem Context

Air Astana operates in a **highly regulated aviation environment**, where internal processes such as procurement and contract approval are subject to strict compliance, audit, and risk requirements.

Despite operational maturity, internal processes remain:
- Highly manual  
- Risk-averse  
- Slow due to personal accountability and fear of errors  

This creates a bottleneck that limits speed and scalability as the organization grows.

---

## 3. Methodology & Structure

The assignment is structured into **three phases**, following modern product management and innovation frameworks.

---

## Phase 1 – Strategic Diagnosis (Cagan Audit)

**Objective:**  
Evaluate whether Air Astana operates as a product-led organization or remains in an **IT Service / project-driven mindset**, particularly for internal operations.

**Approach:**  
- Analyzed the **Air Astana Annual Report 2024** as the sole source of truth  
- Applied Marty Cagan’s Product Operating Model framework:
  - Missionaries vs Mercenaries  
  - Project vs Product funding  
  - IT vs Product  
  - Output vs Outcome  

**Artifacts:**  
- `phase1_cagan_audit/notes.md` – Evidence-based quotes with page numbers  
**Key Insight:**  
While Air Astana is operationally strong, internal processes such as procurement are managed as **control functions**, not as empowered products.

---

## Phase 2 – Deep Discovery (JTBD & 5 Whys)

**Objective:**  
Identify the **real root cause** behind slow procurement processes, beyond surface-level inefficiencies.

**Approach:**  
- Created a synthetic **Senior Procurement Officer persona**
- Conducted a **5 Whys interview simulation**
- Applied the **Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)** framework

**Key Insight:**  
The core problem is **not lack of tools or templates**, but a **risk-averse culture driven by personal accountability and audit pressure**.  
Procurement officers are rewarded for avoiding mistakes, not for speed.

**Artifacts:**  
- `phase2_jtbd/persona_prompt.md` – AI Persona definition  
- `phase2_jtbd/interview_log.md` – 5 Whys interview transcript  
- `phase2_jtbd/prd.md` – Product Requirements Document  

---

## Phase 3 – Managerial Defense (Finance & Governance)

**Objective:**  
Determine whether the proposed AI solution should be built from a **financial and governance perspective**.

**Approach:**  
- Estimated ROI based on time savings, not headcount reduction  
- Evaluated governance, ethics, and regulatory risks specific to aviation  

**Decision Principle:**  
AI acts as **decision-support**, not a decision-maker.

**Artifacts:**  
- `phase3/summary.md` – ROI calculation, risk assessment, Go/No-Go decision  

**Final Decision:**  
**GO (with conditions)**  
The project is financially viable and governance-compliant under strict controls.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- AI transformation succeeds only when **organizational incentives and governance** are addressed
- Internal operations should be treated as **products**, not support functions
- In regulated industries, **trust, auditability, and explainability** matter more than automation speed
- AI delivers the most value as a **risk-reduction and confidence-building tool**
